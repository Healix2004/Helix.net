One of the primary contributors to medical misdiagnosis is the lack of a comprehensive patient medical history at the point of care. Physicians often rely on patients' incomplete memory, which significantly increases the risk of prescribing conflicting medications or missing vital health context. This project addresses these issues by developing a unified, intelligent healthcare platform designed to centralize the medical ecosystem.The proposed system bridges the gap between doctors, patients, pharmacists, and laboratories through a single, integrated digital environment. At its core, the platform leverages advanced Artificial Intelligence to function as a Clinical Decision Support System (CDSS). Additionally, a secondary diagnostic AI model acts as a virtual consultant, analyzing patient data to assist doctors in achieving accurate diagnoses.Technically, the solution facilitates seamless data flow: laboratories can directly upload scans and test results to the patient's record, while pharmacists can access digital prescriptions to dispense medication and issue electronic invoices. Furthermore, the system streamlines administrative workflows through an automated appointment management feature. By consolidating these functions, the project aims to minimize human error, ensure the continuity of medical records, and significantly improve the safety and efficiency of healthcare services.Table of contentsAcknowledgment	iiAbstract	iiiTable of contents	ivList of Figures	x1	Introduction	11.1	General Background	11.2	Problem Statement	11.3	Proposed The integration of advanced technology into medical practices has become essential to improve the quality of care, operational efficiency, and patient safety. Traditionally, medical records have been paper-based and fragmented across disparate clinics, pharmacies, and laboratories, creating a significant barrier to effective treatment. Today, the industry focus is shifting toward centralized, interoperable systems and Artificial Intelligence (AI) to assist clinicians in making precise, data-driven decisions. This project introduces a modern solution to these persistent infrastructural problems by leveraging mobile technology, AI, and international healthcare standards to unify the medical ecosystem.Problem StatementOne of the most critical challenges in the current medical landscape is the absence of a centralized, highly accessible patient history. Patients often visit multiple healthcare providers without carrying their complete medical records and frequently struggle to accurately recall past prescriptions or chronic conditions. This fragmentation results in severe information silos, which serve as a leading cause of misdiagnosis. Furthermore, the manual prescription process carries inherently high risks; a single incorrect medication or an overlooked drug-drug interaction can result in severe adverse drug events (ADEs). Without an intelligent, integrated system to automatically cross-check prescriptions against a patient’s active conditions and concurrent medications, the margin for human error remains dangerously high.Proposed SolutionTo address these critical challenges, we propose Helix, a Unified Medical Platform. This system acts as a comprehensive, centralized hub securely connecting patients, doctors, pharmacists, and laboratories. Unlike traditional, isolated databases, Helix is an intelligent ecosystem architected upon international healthcare data standards (including FHIR R4, SNOMED CT, LOINC, and RxNorm) to guarantee robust global interoperability. The platform is augmented by two core AI microservices:Drug-Drug Interaction (DDI) Model: A predictive AI engine designed to detect conflicts between concurrent medications, actively preventing harmful prescriptions before they are issued.Diagnostic Assistant Chatbot: An AI-driven virtual consultant that analyzes clinical symptoms and historical patient data to assist physicians in formulating accurate, evidence-based diagnoses.Project ObjectivesThe primary objectives of the Helix ecosystem are to:Centralize Medical Records: Engineer a unified, secure profile for each patient containing their comprehensive medical history, diagnostic imaging, and previous clinical treatments.Enhance Patient Safety: Implement intelligent AI algorithms to automatically detect and alert physicians of potential Drug-Drug Interactions (DDIs) in real time.Support Clinical Decision-Making: Equip healthcare providers with AI-driven diagnostic assistance to mitigate misdiagnosis rates and optimize care outcomes.Streamline Clinical Operations: Facilitate secure, end-to-end electronic prescription dispensing for pharmacies and enable direct, standardized diagnostic result archiving for laboratories.Enforce Data Interoperability: Utilize globally recognized medical coding terminologies to ensure strict data consistency and seamless exchange across all participating healthcare providers.Key StakeholdersThis system is meticulously designed to serve four primary end-users within the medical ecosystem:Doctors: To access comprehensive patient histories, utilize AI-driven diagnostic support, issue secure e-prescriptions, and manage clinical workflows efficiently.Patients: To securely store, manage, and share their personal health information through an intuitive mobile interface.Pharmacists: To securely verify, validate, and dispense electronic prescriptions, ensuring medication adherence and safety.Laboratories: To directly upload and archive diagnostic test results and imaging reports into the patient's centralized profile.Business ModelProblem Statement and Market NeedThe Egyptian healthcare sector currently operates within a highly fragmented Information Technology (IT) infrastructure, particularly within the public sector and smaller private clinics. One of the most critical challenges is the lack of standardized Health Information Exchange (HIE) systems, which leads to isolated patient data silos. Medical records, prescriptions, and laboratory results are predominantly paper-based or stored on legacy offline desktop software. This fragmentation forces patients to physically carry their medical history between different healthcare providers, creating a disjointed user journey.Furthermore, the absence of real-time, intelligent clinical decision support systems leaves a significant margin for human error, specifically regarding undetected Drug-Drug Interactions (DDI). This lack of a unified digital ecosystem not only compromises the operational efficiency of healthcare delivery but also creates substantial barriers to achieving seamless data interoperability and proactive patient care on a national scale.The Proposed Solution: HelixTo address these systemic inefficiencies, Helix is introduced as Egypt's first comprehensive, AI-driven medical ecosystem fully compliant with international healthcare data standards, including HL7 and FHIR. Helix serves as a universal bridge for healthcare data, replacing fragmented silos with a robust Health Information Exchange (HIE) architecture. The platform transforms the traditional patient record into a highly secure, digital health passport accessible via a secure QR code sharing protocol, ensuring patient-authorized data access at the point of care.Beyond data digitization, Helix upgrades medical facilities from merely "digitized" to "intelligent" by integrating proprietary Artificial Intelligence models directly into the physician's workflow. This includes a real-time Drug-Drug Interaction (DDI) checker and an AI Diagnostic Assistant, which proactively prevent medical errors and provide advanced clinical decision support. By unifying patients, doctors, and pharmacies within a single interoperable environment, Helix ensures secure workflows while significantly elevating the overall quality, safety, and efficiency of healthcare services.The Business Model CanvasTo ensure the sustainable growth and market viability of the Helix platform, a comprehensive Business Model Canvas (BMC) has been formulated. This strategic management template outlines the fundamental building blocks of the project's economic and operational structure.Value Propositions and Customer SegmentsThe strategic positioning of Helix targets three distinct market segments, offering a tailored Unique Value Proposition (UVP) for each. For Individual Patients (B2C), Helix provides a secure digital health passport managed via QR scanning, alongside a frictionless, zero-commission appointment booking system.  For Pharmaceutical Companies (B2B Data Intelligence), the platform offers real-time market analytics and geographical demand mapping.Revenue Streams and Cost StructureThe financial architecture of Helix relies on a hybrid revenue model. The primary B2B revenue stream is generated through a tiered annual subscription strategy for healthcare facilities, ranging from 90,000 EGP for small clinics up to 250,000 EGP for large-scale hospitals. The cost structure is heavily driven by initial capital requirements for secure hybrid cloud infrastructure, continuous AI model training, and maintaining a highly specialized technical development team. Operational costs also include cloud hosting services (billed in USD) and SMS/OTP gateway fees for patient verification.  Key Infrastructure: Activities, Resources, and PartnershipsMaintaining the Helix ecosystem requires continuous software development, AI algorithm optimization, and strict adherence to the Egyptian Personal Data Protection Law. The platform's key resources are divided into intellectual assets (proprietary AI algorithms) and physical/technological infrastructure (high-availability localized data centers and cloud services). Strategic partnerships are crucial, particularly with global Cloud Service Providers (e.g., AWS, Azure) for scalable hosting, and telecommunication gateways for secure verification.  Channels and Customer RelationshipsHelix employs a multi-channel approach to reach its target audience. Many follow-up appointments, consultations, and medication reviews do not require the physical presence of the patient, and requiring patients to travel for such visits adds unnecessary cost and delay. To address this need, the Healix platform includes a dedicated video call microservice that allows doctors and patients to hold secure, real-time video consultations directly from their web browsers.The service complements the rest of the platform naturally: after a doctor issues an electronic prescription or reviews laboratory results, a short video consultation is often all that is needed to explain the treatment plan, answer the patient's questions, or confirm that the medication is working as intended. Instead of relying on external tools such as Zoom or WhatsApp, which are outside the control of the platform, Healix provides this capability as a first-class part of the system.The service was designed around three goals: simplicity, privacy, and independence. Simplicity means that a consultation can be started and joined without installing any software or creating an additional account. Privacy means that the audio and video content of a consultation is never stored on, or routed through, the platform's servers. Independence means that the service is built and deployed as a self-contained microservice that can be scaled, updated, and maintained without affecting the rest of the Healix system.System OverviewThe microservice provides one-to-one video meetings between a doctor and a patient. The doctor creates a meeting room and receives a shareable invitation link, which is then sent to the patient. When the patient opens the link, the browser requests access to the camera and microphone, and the call is established within a few seconds. Each participant is identified only by a display name chosen at the start of the session.A typical consultation proceeds as follows: the doctor opens the home page, enters a name for the session, and creates the room. The call page opens immediately and indicates that the system is waiting for the other participant to join. The doctor copies the invitation link and sends it to the patient through any convenient channel. As soon as the patient opens the link and grants camera access, both sides see and hear each other and the session status changes to connected. The main capabilities of the service are:Creation of meeting rooms with human-readable session namesJoining a call through an invitation link or by entering the room identifierLive text chat displayed beside the video during the callMicrophone muting and camera toggling at any point in the sessionImmediate notification when the other participant joins or leaves the roomEach meeting room accepts a maximum of two participants; any additional attempt to join is rejected with a clear indication that the room is full. Meeting information is held entirely in memory and no recordings, messages, or session details are persisted to a database. This is a deliberate design decision: because nothing is stored, a finished consultation leaves no recoverable media behind, which strengthens the confidentiality of the doctor-patient interaction.Development Tools and TechnologiesThe backend of the service is implemented in Java 17 using the Spring Boot framework, combining the Spring Web starter for the REST endpoints with the Spring WebSocket starter for real-time messaging. Java records, switch expressions, and immutable data structures are used throughout the codebase to keep the implementation concise and safe.Real-time media transmission is based on WebRTC, the web standard for browser-to-browser communication. The browser side of the service uses the RTCPeerConnection and getUserMedia APIs together with a public STUN server, which allows the two browsers to discover a direct network path to each other even when they are located behind routers or firewalls.The user interface is served by the same application as a set of static HTML, CSS, and plain JavaScript pages, without any frontend framework. This keeps the deployable unit small and removes an entire class of dependency and build complexity that a framework would introduce for what is, by design, a focused single-purpose interface.Architecture and Code StructureThe service is organized into three cooperating parts. The REST layer exposes a small API for creating a meeting and for checking that a meeting exists before a participant attempts to join it. The signaling layer is a WebSocket handler through which the two browsers exchange the technical messages required to establish a call. The state layer is an in-memory room registry that tracks which sessions are currently present in which room using thread-safe data structures, so that concurrent joins and disconnections are handled correctly.The architecture of the service and the separation between the signaling path and the media path are illustrated in the figure below./Figure ‎71 Video Call Microservice ArchitectureSignaling WorkflowA video consultation follows a well-defined sequence of steps. First, the doctor creates a meeting through the REST API, which generates a random room identifier and returns a shareable join link. Both participants then open the call page, and each browser establishes a WebSocket connection to the signaling endpoint and sends a join request for the room. All signaling traffic is expressed as small JSON messages, each carrying one of a fixed set of message types:join, offer, answer, ice, chat, and leave — sent by the browsersjoined, peer-joined, peer-left, room-full, and not-found — sent by the backendThe backend validates every join request before admitting the participant: if the room identifier does not correspond to an existing meeting, the request is rejected, and if the room already contains two participants, the newcomer is informed that the room is full. Once both participants are admitted, the browsers negotiate the call by exchanging an offer, an answer, and a series of network candidate descriptions. The backend acts purely as a relay for these messages and does not interpret or modify their content.When the negotiation completes, an encrypted media channel is established directly between the two browsers, and audio and video begin to flow peer-to-peer. Text chat messages are relayed through the same WebSocket channel. When a participant leaves the call or closes the browser, the backend removes the session from the room and immediately notifies the remaining participant, so the other side is never left waiting without feedback.Design PatternsSeveral established design patterns shape the implementation of the service. The backend follows the signaling server pattern that underpins WebRTC applications: it coordinates how a call is set up but deliberately stays out of the media path. The room registry implements the registry pattern, providing a single, thread-safe authority on room membership with atomic join and leave operations.All signaling messages are modeled as immutable Java records created through static factory methods, which prevents accidental modification of messages as they pass through the system. Incoming messages are routed by a type-based dispatch mechanism, in which an enumeration of message types combined with a switch expression directs each message to its handler. Finally, all dependencies are provided through constructor injection, which keeps the components loosely coupled and straightforward to test.Challenges and SolutionsEstablishing a direct connection between two browsers is not trivial, because most devices sit behind home routers or mobile networks that hide their real network addresses. The service solves this through the standard WebRTC approach: a STUN server helps each browser discover how it is seen from the public internet, and the candidate addresses gathered this way are exchanged through the signaling channel until a working path is found.Another challenge is the possibility of two participants joining the same room at the same instant. If room membership were checked and updated in two separate steps, a race condition could briefly allow three participants into a two-person room. The room registry therefore performs the capacity check and the insertion as a single atomic operation, which makes the limit reliable even under concurrent access.Finally, real networks are unreliable: a participant may lose connectivity or simply close the browser without pressing the leave button. The backend treats an unexpected connection loss exactly like an explicit departure — the session is removed from its room and the remaining participant is notified immediately — so no room is ever left in an inconsistent state.Security ConsiderationsThe security model of the video call service follows the same principles applied across the Healix platform. Room identifiers are generated with a cryptographically secure random generator, so meeting rooms cannot be guessed or enumerated by an outside party; similar to the QR code design described earlier, the identifier itself carries no sensitive information. The two-participant capacity limit is enforced on the server for every join request, preventing an uninvited third party from silently entering an ongoing consultation.Media confidentiality is guaranteed by the WebRTC standard itself, which mandates end-to-end encryption of all audio and video streams. Because the media flows directly between the two browsers, the platform's servers never have access to the content of a consultation. Combined with the absence of any persistent storage, this means that no recording, transcript, or message history exists after a call ends. In production deployments the service is served exclusively over HTTPS, which browsers additionally require before granting access to the camera and microphone.DeploymentThe service is packaged with a multi-stage Docker build. The first stage compiles the application with Maven, while the final stage copies only the resulting artifact into a slim image based on a minimal Java runtime and runs it as a dedicated non-root user, following container security best practices. The result is a single self-contained image that can be started with one command on any host that runs Docker.Because the service keeps all of its state in memory and depends on no database or message broker, deployment and scaling remain simple: instances can be started, replaced, or upgraded independently of the rest of the Healix platform, and a restart simply clears any rooms that are no longer in use.TestingThe service is verified by two automated test suites. The first suite exercises the complete signaling protocol against a running instance, covering meeting creation, joining, message relay, chat delivery, room capacity enforcement, unknown room handling, and participant departure, for a total of twenty checks. The second suite performs a real end-to-end call between two headless browsers with simulated cameras and verifies that the peer connection is established, that video frames are rendered on both sides, that chat messages are delivered in both directions, and that the mute, camera, and leave controls behave correctly, for a total of sixteen checks.Running the end-to-end suite with two different browsers on the same machine also validates a subtle real-world scenario: a physical webcam can be used by only one application at a time. The service handles this gracefully by allowing the second participant to join with microphone only, which mirrors the behavior a patient would experience on a device without a camera.SummaryThe video call microservice extends Healix with secure, real-time video consultations while remaining fully independent from the rest of the platform. By combining a lightweight Spring Boot signaling backend with peer-to-peer WebRTC media transmission, the service delivers private doctor-patient communication with no stored media, no additional accounts, and no software installation, and its in-memory, stateless design keeps it simple to deploy, scale, and maintain.Artificial Intelligence (DDIMDL Model ) IntroductionThe file In many traditional systems, patient records, prescriptions, laboratory results, and radiology reports are stored separately, which can delay access to important information and increase the risk of medical errors. Helix was designed to solve this problem by connecting patients, doctors, pharmacists, laboratories, and administrators within one secure digital platform.The system centralizes the patient’s medical data, including personal information, chronic diseases, allergies, diagnoses, medications, appointments, prescriptions, laboratory results, radiology reports, and uploaded documents. This allows healthcare professionals to access a more complete medical history and make better-informed decisions. It also enables patients to manage their health information and share it securely with authorized providers.Helix provides separate role-based dashboards for each user type. Patients can book appointments, view prescriptions, follow test results, and access their medical records. Doctors can review patient histories, manage appointments, create diagnoses, issue electronic prescriptions, and request laboratory or radiology services. Pharmacists can verify and dispense prescriptions, while laboratories can receive requests and upload results directly to patient records. Administrators can manage users, services, and platform activities.The platform was developed using modern technologies. Angular and TypeScript were used for the web application, Flutter was used for the mobile application, and ASP.NET Core with RESTful APIs was used for the back-end. SQL Server was used for data storage, while the system architecture was organized into separate layers to improve scalability, maintainability, and testing. The project also applies healthcare standards such as FHIR, SNOMED CT, LOINC, and RxNorm to improve the consistency and interoperability of medical data.Artificial Intelligence is one of the main features of the project. The Drug-Drug Interaction model analyzes prescribed medications and compares them with the patient’s current drugs. If a possible harmful interaction is detected, the system alerts the doctor before the prescription is finalized. The platform also includes a medical chatbot assistant that provides intelligent support based on medical information and patient data. These tools are intended to assist healthcare professionals and improve patient safety, while the final clinical decision remains the responsibility of the doctor.Security and privacy were also essential parts of the implementation. Helix uses authentication, email verification, JWT-based sessions, protected routes, and role-based authorization to control access to sensitive medical data. The system also includes secure QR-code sharing, input validation, and controlled access to records. These mechanisms help reduce unauthorized access and protect patient information.The user interface was designed to be clear, responsive, and easy to use. Consistent navigation, validation messages, loading indicators, and error feedback were applied across the system. Responsive design allows the platform to work effectively on desktops, laptops, tablets, and mobile devices. By organizing complex healthcare tasks into clear steps, Helix improves the experience of both patients and healthcare professionals.During development, the team faced several challenges, including integrating multiple user roles, connecting the front-end with the back-end, managing authentication and authorization, handling file uploads, applying healthcare terminology, and integrating AI services. Solving these challenges strengthened the overall system architecture and provided valuable experience in software engineering, healthcare systems, cloud technologies, and teamwork.In conclusion, Helix demonstrates the feasibility of building a secure, scalable, and AI-supported healthcare platform. It provides a strong foundation for centralizing medical records, improving communication between healthcare providers, reducing medication-related risks, and supporting safer clinical decisions. Although further clinical validation, security testing, and regulatory approval would be required before real-world deployment, the project represents an important step toward a more connected, efficient, and patient-centered digital healthcare environment.ReferencesAung, S. أجهزة الكمبيوتر والهواتف المحمولة والأجهزة اللوحية. ويشمل النظام نسخة ويب بالإضافة إلى تطبيق للهواتف المحمولة، بما يتيح للمستخدم الوصول إلى الخدمات الطبية من أكثر من وسيلة.تم تطوير الواجهة الأمامية للمنصة باستخدام إطار العمل Angular ولغة TypeScript، مع استخدام HTML5 وCSS3 وTailwind CSS لإنشاء واجهات حديثة ومتجاوبة. أما تطبيق الهاتف المحمول فقد تم تنظيمه باستخدام بنية قابلة للتوسع تعتمد على فصل الخصائص والمكونات. وتم تطوير الخدمات الخلفية باستخدام تقنيات ASP.NET Core و.NET، مع الاعتماد على واجهات برمجية RESTful APIs لربط أجزاء النظام المختلفة. كما يدعم المشروع المعايير الدولية لتبادل البيانات الطبية، مثل HL7 وFHIR، مما يساهم في توحيد شكل البيانات وتسهيل التكامل مستقبلًا مع المستشفيات والأنظمة الطبية الأخرى.اهتم المشروع بدرجة كبيرة بأمن البيانات، نظرًا لحساسية المعلومات الطبية. يستخدم النظام تسجيل الدخول والتحقق من البريد الإلكتروني بواسطة رمز OTP، إلى جانب استخدام رموز JWT للتحقق من هوية المستخدم وحماية الطلبات المرسلة إلى الخادم. كما يتم تطبيق نظام الصلاحيات المبني على الأدوار، بحيث لا يستطيع كل مستخدم الوصول إلا إلى الصفحات والبيانات المسموح بها. ويتضمن النظام كذلك التحقق من البيانات المدخلة، وحماية عمليات مشاركة السجلات الطبية، وتأمين رموز QR، وتسجيل العمليات المهمة بهدف المتابعة والمراجعة.وعلى مستوى النشر والتشغيل، يعتمد المشروع على استراتيجية حديثة في الحوسبة السحابية وعمليات DevOps. تم تجهيز مكونات الواجهة الأمامية والخدمات الخلفية وخدمات الذكاء الاصطناعي للعمل داخل حاويات Docker، مع استخدام خدمات AWS، وأدوات مثل Terraform لإدارة البنية التحتية، وJenkins لتنفيذ عمليات التكامل والنشر المستمر، وKubernetes وAWS EKS لإدارة الحاويات وتوفير قابلية التوسع والاستقرار. تساعد هذه البنية على تشغيل المنصة بكفاءة، وتقليل الأعطال، وتحديث النظام بصورة أسرع وأكثر أمانًا.في النهاية، يقدم مشروع Helix حلًا متكاملًا يهدف إلى تحسين جودة الرعاية الصحية من خلال توحيد البيانات الطبية وربط جميع مقدمي الخدمات داخل منصة واحدة. ويساهم المشروع في تقليل الاعتماد على الملفات الورقية، وتقليل الأخطاء الناتجة عن نقص المعلومات أو تعارض الأدوية، وتسريع التواصل بين الأطباء والمعامل والصيدليات، ومنح المريض قدرة أكبر على متابعة حالته الصحية. وبذلك يمثل Helix نموذجًا لمنظومة صحية رقمية ذكية تجمع بين سهولة الاستخدام، وأمن البيانات، والذكاء الاصطناعي، والمعايير الطبية الحديثة، بهدف تقديم خدمات صحية أكثر دقة وكفاءة وأمانًا.منظومة طبية متكاملة متكاملة مدعومة بالذكاء الاصطناعيالمشرفين:أستاذ الدكتور محمد معوضأستاذة مساعد
